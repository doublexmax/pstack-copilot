### Autopilot-full

**You own the verdicts, never the PRs. One owner runs each PR from build to merge, and nothing merges without your clean swarm verdict.** For "autopilot this queue", "full autopilot", and one-owner-per-PR programs. The job is a queue of independent PRs handed over to drive to merged with full autonomy. Orchestrate runs a standing program whose coordinator lands verified work itself and whose workers never merge. Here each PR's owner carries the whole lifecycle through the merge, and the root keeps only verification, countersigns, and audits. ADO mechanics are in `../references/ado.md`.

1. **Mark the operator's items and honor state-then-wait.** Items the operator names stay with the operator. The operator reviews and clicks, and no owner merges one. When the operator asks for the protocol or the plan to be stated, deliver the statement and stop. Execution starts only on the operator's explicit go.
2. **Spawn one owner per PR with the full lifecycle and an early trail.** One cloud session per PR (`create_session` with `execution_location: "cloud"`) owns build, the first push, opening its PR, self-proof on the real artifact (the **prove-it-works** principle skill), skeptical review-bot triage per `../references/bugbot-triage.md`, a slop-strip with the **unslop** skill, `/no-comments` (the **no-comments** skill), a rebase onto current `master`, the babysit loop to green (`playbooks/babysit.md`), and the merge itself. Within about 15 minutes, every owner starts a `decisions.tsv` trail per the **show-me-your-work** skill, pushes its first branch snapshot, and opens the PR ready, never draft. After that it pushes again after every verifiable unit, hooks on, and a WIP commit is fine. Opening the PR before self-proof makes the URL, the decisions, and the checks a durable trail. Keep `decisions.tsv` uncommitted and return it with the reports. As soon as a subagent starts, the owner adds its ID, its expected runtime (at least the longest past run of that kind), and its state to a `children.tsv` kept the same way. The first rebase comes before the code-ready report and babysit, whether or not `master` drifted, and fix rounds keep that merge base. Rebase again only at merge prep (step 5), on a `git merge-tree` conflict with `master`, or on a CI failure that comes from a change on `master`. When the shipped code is final, after the slop-strip and `/no-comments`, the owner reports the code-ready head SHA, and the SHA of every later push that changes the patch. Self-proof, CI, and babysit then run in parallel with the swarm, and the owner reports merge-ready with the head SHA when all three finish. Before a push that starts a round, run the pre-review checks the repo's `AGENTS.md` files and rules name for the touched paths, on the committed head. A hook pass is not proof. Publish each rebase by pushing the owner's own branch with `git push --force-with-lease` after an `ls-remote` check, and never force-push a shared branch. The merge is the one step an owner may not take alone. Step 4 gates it.
3. **Run owners in true parallel and never chain.** Many owners at once when PRs are self-contained: one writer per branch, disjoint files, cross-PR drift absorbed by rebase. Only genuinely overlapping work serializes. Self-contained PRs branch straight off `master` and target it directly, so every PR carries the repo's real policy set and no PR can complete into an unprotected branch. Sequenced work is merge-then-branch. One exception: an owner that must split a genuinely dependent change may hold a short private chain, and then only the root writes its target refs.
4. **Swarm-verify every round before its merge.** A round starts at the owner's code-ready head SHA and at each later push that changes the PR's patch. At that SHA, fan out parallel independent verifiers per the **swarm** skill and aggregate to one verdict. The fan-out mechanics live there; do not restate them. The merge needs a clean verdict from the round whose patch matches the merge-ready head, and the root audits the receipts in the merge-ready report before the verdict. The lanes: re-run the gates at that SHA. Prove the load-bearing behavior live on the real surface the change touches, using the project's own `.github/skills/verify-*` skill where one exists, or a named driver where none does. Audit the diff and the receipts, distrusting the PR body. Run the audit as two or more review lanes with the full brief, each with one main focus, such as consumer parity with `master`, lifetimes and races, or data and config safety. One lane is the regression lane against `master`. It runs the same load-bearing scenario there. If `master` does not have the feature, the lane records that fact and gates the behavior the diff adds plus the end state the user waits for, rather than pretending `master` can produce it. The live lane is the floor, and a verdict without it is not clean. No merge without the root's clean verdict. When the lanes return, send every proven finding against the PR to the owner in one fix-forward. A defect a lane filed as a note is a finding. For each behavior finding, ask for a red test that covers every site with the same defect, or a repro receipt where no test can show it, and add that defect to the next round's review brief. The new head gets a fresh swarm and a fresh verdict, except for lane results that stay valid under the patch-id rule in `playbooks/shipping.md`.
5. **On a clean verdict the owner merges, and a fresh owner takes the next item.** The owner merges only from a head freshly rebased on `master`. Merge prep never comes before a round's lanes start, and it ends with a rebase onto current `master` right before the merge. After that rebase the owner reports the new head SHA, CI must pass on it, and the patch-id rule in `playbooks/shipping.md` decides whether the round's verdict still holds. Once that head is green and its patch-id matches the verdict's, a later `master` move does not force another rebase. Right before the merge, fetch `master` and check that `git merge-tree` of the head against it is clean, and that no path in `git diff --name-only $(git merge-base HEAD origin/master) origin/master` is a path the PR changes or a path that decides which CI runs for it, such as the repo's pipeline definitions. If either check fails, rebase again, report the new head SHA, wait for CI, and repeat these checks. A new head voids the verdict unless the patch-id is unchanged. The owner completes its own PR with a squash merge, arming auto-complete only after the verdict is in hand and confirming `autoCompleteSetBy` came back populated, then returns. A fresh owner picks up the next self-contained item from the queue, per poteto-mode's Subagents section. The operator's full-autonomy grant plus the root's clean verdict is the merge authorization that babysitting alone never has. Operator-named items stop at merge-ready and wait for the operator's click.
6. **Run the root layer.** A genuinely new raise of a pinned gate or budget value (a limit CI only lets tighten) needs your fresh countersign, granted only after verifier proof. If the operator's grant or standing orders cover approvals, that countersign is the approval. The owner records it in the form the tool's approval contract allows, with a pointer to the root's countersign, and a lane checks the record against that countersign. The root never gives or bypasses an approval the forge enforces. Absorbing values that already landed on `master` is drift, not a raise. Run an audit tick over all owners every hour. On the operator's go, arm the cadence with `save_session_automation` at `interval: "minutes"` and `every_minutes: 60`, so it survives a turn ending. Never leave the cadence to memory or to lossy completion notifications. At each tick, re-read this playbook from trunk with `git show origin/master:skills/poteto-mode/playbooks/autopilot-full.md` and audit the operation against it, fixing drift during that tick. Read each owner with `get_session`, probe liveness, and collect the decision trails. Count only side effects as progress: commits, pushes, PR or check deltas, and stored reports. Treat a lane that errors, or that passes its expected runtime without a side effect, as stuck. Stand it down and dispatch a replacement at once rather than waiting for a polite return. The tick judges an owner by its pushed branch and the trail step 2 requires, and replaces an owner whose session cannot start a turn. Each tick also runs the same stuck test over every owner's `children.tsv`. Whether or not a stop lands, have the owner record each stuck subagent as stuck and replace it when its work is still needed, and take both steps yourself when the owner cannot. A stall never proves or drops the work. When merges batch, run a retro pass and a post-merge bot-comment sweep. End the tick only when no delegated work is left, even after the last merge.
7. **Stand down instantly on the operator's stop.** The operator's hold or stand-down reaches every owner as a zero-writes order immediately, sent with `send_session_message` at `delivery_mode: "immediate"` so it lands mid-turn rather than queuing behind the work you are trying to stop. Owners hold their briefs until the operator releases them.

**Reply:** the queue with each PR's owner, state, and head SHA. Each verdict and the swarm that produced it. What merged and what each fresh owner took next. Countersigns granted and why. Open operator gates. Where the collected decision trails live.
